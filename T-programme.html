Fuel Your Progress

Nutrition
Guide

Food is the biggest lever in fat loss — roughly 80% of results come from what you eat. The goal is high protein, balanced healthy carbs, and meals you genuinely enjoy. These aren't strict rules — they're smart combinations to build around your preferred foods.

⚠️ Allergy Alert: This plan strictly avoids almonds and tree nuts in all forms — including almond flour, almond milk, mixed nuts, and pesto. Always check labels. When eating out, flag your allergy with the restaurant.

🔢 Loose Calorie Tracking — Super Simple

Think of calories like a daily budget. You don't need to count every single thing — just be aware of the big picture. Here's all you need to know:

  • Your rough daily target: Around 1,400–1,600 calories to lose fat steadily. Eat more on gym days, less on rest days — naturally.
  • Protein = 80–100g per day. Each gram of protein = 4 calories. Aim for a palm-sized portion of protein at every meal.
  • Plate rule: Half the plate = veg. Quarter = protein. Quarter = healthy carbs (sweet potato, oats, rice). Add a thumb-sized amount of healthy fat (avocado, olive oil).
  • Don't stress the numbers — if you're eating whole foods, hitting protein, and not drinking calories, you're already doing it right.

🥩 Preferred Proteins

  • NY Strip / Sirloin Steak — ~250–300 cal for a 6oz cut, ~35g protein. Grill or pan sear. Pair with sweet potato and salad. Iron-rich and deeply satisfying.
  • Sardines — one of the best fat-loss foods around. ~200 cal per tin, ~25g protein, loaded with omega-3. On salad, on crackers, or straight from the tin.
  • Beef Mince (Ground Beef) — use lean 5% fat or extra-lean. ~200 cal per 100g, ~26g protein. Season simply with garlic, cumin, paprika. Use in lettuce wraps, bowls, or with sweet potato mash.
  • Eggs — ~70 cal each, ~6g protein. Scramble, boil, or make a 3-egg omelette. Quick, cheap, filling.
  • Chicken breast or thigh — your reliable everyday protein. Grill, bake, or cook in mince-style for variety.

🍠 Healthy Carbs — Your Energy Source

Carbs aren't the enemy — the wrong carbs at the wrong time are. These are your go-to options:

  • Sweet Potato ⭐ — ~100 cal per medium potato, high in fibre, keeps you full for hours. Roast, mash, or slice into wedges. Best eaten post-workout when your body uses the carbs for recovery.
  • Oats — slow-release energy, perfect for breakfast. ~150 cal per portion. Use as the base for your yoghurt bowls.
  • Brown rice or quinoa — use in small portions (½ cup cooked) alongside protein and veg.
  • Fruit — natural sugars are fine in moderation. Berries (lowest sugar), banana (great pre-workout), mango for a treat.

🫙 Greek Yoghurt Bowls — A Go-To Meal

These are perfect as breakfast, a post-workout snack, or dessert. Mix and match:

  • Base: Full-fat or 2% Greek yoghurt (~150–180 cal, ~15–20g protein per cup) — always check it's nut-free
  • Sweetener: A drizzle of honey (~20 cal per tsp) — keeps it natural and delicious
  • Oats: 2–3 tbsp rolled oats for texture and slow-release carbs (~60 cal)
  • Fruit combos: Strawberries + honey · Blueberries + oats · Mango + a pinch of cinnamon · Banana + oats (great pre-gym)
  • Add-ins: Chia seeds, a pinch of cinnamon, sunflower seeds (nut-safe) for extra texture

🍵 Recommended Teas

  • Black Tea — zero calories, gentle caffeine boost for energy and focus. Great pre-workout or with breakfast.
  • Green Tea — contains EGCG, a compound shown to mildly support fat burning. 2–3 cups per day is ideal. Excellent between meals.
  • Spearmint Tea — caffeine-free, great for digestion and reducing bloating. Perfect after dinner or before bed.
  • All three count toward your 2L daily fluid target. Keep them unsweetened or with just a tiny bit of honey.

🍽️ Recommended Meal Combos

These aren't a strict meal plan — pick what works for you that day.

  • Morning: Greek yoghurt bowl (oats + berries + honey) + black or green tea
  • Pre-gym: Banana + Greek yoghurt, or oats with honey 60–90 min before
  • Post-gym lunch: Sirloin steak + roasted sweet potato wedges + side salad with olive oil
  • Easy lunch: Sardines on salad (romaine, cucumber, cherry tomatoes, lemon dressing) — no cooking needed
  • Dinner: Beef mince bowl — seasoned mince, sweet potato mash, steamed broccoli or green beans
  • Evening snack: Greek yoghurt + honey + cinnamon, or 2 boiled eggs, or spearmint tea if not hungry

💧 Daily Non-Negotiables

  • 2L+ still water daily — start every morning with a full glass before anything else. Still water is gentler on digestion than sparkling.
  • Protein at every meal — this is the single most important rule for fat loss and keeping hunger under control.
  • No sugary drinks — swap fizzy drinks for still water, herbal teas, or water with fresh lemon (avoid carbonated soft drinks where possible).
  • Eat slowly and chew properly — take at least 15–20 minutes per meal. Your body absorbs far more nutrients, you feel fuller on less food, and your gut works much more comfortably.
  • 3 meals + 1 snack — skipping meals usually leads to overeating later and disrupts gut rhythm.
  • Sleep 7–8 hours — this is when fat is actually burned, muscle is built, and your digestive system fully resets.
  • Support your gut: Greek yoghurt, kefir, and fermented foods contain probiotics — directly linked to better metabolism, mood, and digestion.

How Food Affects You

Food & Energy Levels

Different foods hit your body at different speeds. Think of it like fuel grades — some burn fast, some burn slow.

⚡
Fast Energy

Fruit, honey, white rice, bananas. These digest quickly and give you a burst of energy — great before a workout (30–60 min prior). The downside: they fade fast and can leave you hungry sooner.

🔋
Slow Energy

Sweet potato, oats, Greek yoghurt, brown rice. These release energy steadily for hours — ideal for breakfast or lunch. They keep you fuller longer and prevent energy crashes.

🏗️
Building Blocks

Steak, sardines, eggs, mince, Greek yoghurt. Protein doesn't give you a quick energy hit — it repairs and builds muscle after training and keeps hunger under control all day.

🧠
Brain & Mood Food

Sardines, eggs, avocado, olive oil. Healthy fats keep your brain sharp, support hormone balance, and actually help your body burn stored fat more efficiently. Don't skip them.

Recovery & Rest

Training Recovery

Getting fitter doesn't happen during your workout — it happens in the hours afterwards. Recovery is where the real progress is made.

😴
Sleep

7–8 hours is non-negotiable. Growth hormone (which burns fat AND builds muscle) is mostly released during deep sleep. Bad sleep = slower results, more cravings, lower energy.

💧
Hydration

Muscles are ~75% water. Being even slightly dehydrated reduces strength and endurance by up to 10%. Drink 2L+ daily, more on gym days.

🍽️
Post-Workout Meal

Eat within 60–90 minutes of training. Protein + a healthy carb (like sweet potato) is the ideal combo — protein repairs, carbs replenish.

🚶‍♀️
Active Rest

On rest days, light walking is better than doing nothing. It keeps blood flowing to sore muscles, reduces stiffness, and burns extra calories without stressing the body.

Recovery Methods

Beyond the Gym — How to Recover

The best athletes in the world spend as much time on recovery as they do on training. These tools aren't luxuries — they're part of the programme. Use what's accessible to you and build from there.

Heat Therapy
🧖‍♀️ Sauna
▾
  • What happens: Heat causes blood vessels to dilate, dramatically increasing blood flow to muscles. This flushes out waste products (like lactic acid) that build up during exercise and delivers fresh oxygen and nutrients to recovering tissue.
  • Fat loss benefit: A 20-minute sauna session can raise your heart rate similarly to a light walk. Over time, regular sauna use is linked to improved cardiovascular health and increased growth hormone — the hormone responsible for fat burning and muscle repair.
  • Soreness relief: The heat relaxes muscle fibres and reduces the stiffness felt 24–48 hours after a hard session (called DOMS — delayed onset muscle soreness).
  • Mental benefit: Sauna triggers endorphin release — the same feel-good chemicals as exercise. Great for stress reduction and mental reset.
  • When to use: Best on rest days or after a training session (not before). Start with 10–15 minutes at a comfortable heat. Stay hydrated — drink water before and after.
  • How often: Even once a week makes a meaningful difference. 2–3 times is ideal if accessible.
Heat Therapy
💨 Steam Room
▾
  • What happens: Similar to a sauna but uses moist heat (100% humidity). The steam opens up airways and pores, making it particularly effective for respiratory health and skin.
  • Recovery benefit: The moist heat penetrates muscles more gently than dry sauna heat — great for people who find saunas too intense. Loosens tight muscles and joints effectively.
  • Breathing benefit: Steam inhalation clears airways and can improve oxygen delivery — directly supporting cardio endurance over time.
  • Skin & detox: Opens pores and promotes sweating, which helps clear skin and supports the body's natural detox processes. Especially noticeable after a few weeks of consistency.
  • When to use: After training, or on rest days. 10–15 minutes is enough. Don't stay in if you feel dizzy or too hot.
  • Sauna vs Steam: Both are excellent. Sauna = slightly more intense, better for muscle recovery and growth hormone. Steam = gentler, better for breathing, skin, and joint stiffness. Use whichever is available — or alternate.
Bodywork
💆‍♀️ Massage
▾
  • What happens: Massage physically breaks up adhesions (knots) in muscle tissue, increases localised blood flow, and stimulates the lymphatic system — which removes waste and reduces swelling.
  • Soreness relief: Shown in studies to significantly reduce DOMS (that deep aching soreness 1–2 days after training). A sports massage within 24 hours of a hard session can cut recovery time noticeably.
  • Performance benefit: Regular massage keeps muscle tissue healthy, elastic, and less prone to injury. Over a 15-week programme, this matters a lot.
  • Mental benefit: Reduces cortisol (stress hormone) and raises serotonin and dopamine — improving mood, motivation, and sleep quality.
  • Types to consider: Sports massage (deeper, targets specific muscles), Swedish massage (full-body relaxation, great for stress), or even a self-massage with a foam roller or massage gun at home.
  • How often: Once every 2–4 weeks professionally is ideal. Self-massage with a foam roller after every session is free and very effective.
Cold Therapy
🧊 Cold Shower / Ice Bath
▾
  • What happens: Cold causes blood vessels to constrict, reducing inflammation and swelling in muscle tissue. When you warm up again, vessels dilate and rush fresh blood to the area — like a pumping action for recovery.
  • Soreness relief: Cold exposure after intense training reduces acute muscle soreness and swelling. Athletes have used ice baths for decades for this reason.
  • Mental benefit: Cold showers trigger a large release of noradrenaline — a neurotransmitter linked to focus, energy, and mood. Even a 30-second cold blast at the end of a warm shower is enough to feel the effect.
  • Fat loss bonus: Cold exposure activates brown fat tissue — a type of fat that burns calories to generate heat. Regular cold exposure can modestly boost metabolism over time.
  • How to start: Don't jump straight into an ice bath. Start by ending your normal shower with 30 seconds of cold water. Gradually extend to 2–3 minutes over a few weeks. Take slow, controlled breaths — don't panic.
  • Note: Avoid cold therapy immediately before training — it can blunt performance. Best used post-training or on rest days.
Bodywork
🛞 Foam Rolling
▾
  • What happens: Foam rolling is a form of self-myofascial release — it puts pressure on tight spots in the muscle and fascia (the connective tissue around muscles), helping them relax and lengthen.
  • Why it works: Sitting, training, and everyday movement creates tension knots in muscles. Rolling over these areas stimulates sensory receptors that tell the nervous system to relax the tissue.
  • Best areas to roll: Quads, hamstrings, glutes, upper back, and calves — especially after leg day or any session involving squats, lunges, or hip thrusts.
  • How to do it: Slow, controlled rolls — when you find a tender spot, pause and hold gentle pressure for 20–30 seconds rather than rolling quickly over it. That pause is where the release happens.
  • When to use: After training as part of your cooldown, or on rest days for 5–10 minutes while watching TV. Zero cost and can be done at home.
  • Equipment: A basic foam roller costs £10–20 and lasts years. A massage gun is great if available but not essential.
Daily Habit
🛁 Epsom Salt Bath
▾
  • What happens: Epsom salts are magnesium sulphate. Soaking in warm water with Epsom salts allows magnesium to be absorbed through the skin — and magnesium is one of the most important minerals for muscle relaxation and recovery.
  • Why it matters: Most people are mildly deficient in magnesium, especially those who exercise. Low magnesium = more muscle cramps, worse sleep, higher stress levels, and slower recovery.
  • Recovery benefit: Reduces muscle cramps, eases tension, and promotes deep relaxation. The warm water also provides the same circulatory benefits as a steam room.
  • Sleep benefit: A warm bath 1–2 hours before bed has been shown to improve sleep onset and quality — partly from the magnesium, partly from the body cooling down afterwards (which signals sleep time).
  • How to use: Add 2 cups of Epsom salts to a warm bath. Soak for 20 minutes. Available at any pharmacy for a few pounds.
  • When to use: After a hard training session, or on a rest day evening before bed. A simple, inexpensive recovery tool most people overlook.
Daily Habit
🧘‍♀️ Breathwork & Meditation
▾
  • What happens: Slow, controlled breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system — your body's "rest and digest" mode. This is the opposite of the "fight or flight" state your body enters during exercise and stress.
  • Recovery benefit: Getting into parasympathetic mode faster after training accelerates recovery, reduces cortisol, and improves how efficiently your body repairs muscle tissue.
  • Fat loss link: High cortisol (chronic stress) directly causes the body to store fat — particularly around the midsection. Managing stress is a real, underrated fat loss tool.
  • Simple technique — Box Breathing: Inhale for 4 counts → hold for 4 → exhale for 4 → hold for 4. Repeat 5–8 times. Takes 3 minutes. Immediately calming.
  • Apps to try: Headspace, Calm, or simply a YouTube "5-minute meditation" search. You don't need to sit cross-legged — lying on your back works perfectly.
  • When to use: After training as part of cooldown, before bed, or any time you feel stressed or overwhelmed. Even 5 minutes makes a measurable difference.

📋 Recovery Quick Reference — What to Use & When

  • After a hard gym session: Foam roll, cold shower, protein meal within 90 min
  • Very sore muscles next day: Epsom salt bath, gentle walk, ginger tea
  • Rest day recovery: Sauna or steam room, full body stretch flow, chamomile tea before bed
  • Feeling stressed or can't switch off: Box breathing, chamomile tea, warm bath
  • Low budget options: Cold shower + foam roller + Epsom salts — all under £20 total and highly effective
  • Treat yourself option: Sports massage every 2–4 weeks — worth every penny for recovery and motivation

Miss One, Not Two

Everyone misses a session. The rule is: never miss twice in a row. One missed session is a blip. Two becomes a pattern. Three becomes quitting.

🍽️
Prep Once, Eat Well All Week

Cook a batch of mince, roast a tray of sweet potatoes, boil some eggs on Sunday. Having food ready removes the biggest excuse to eat badly.

The Bottom Line

You don't need to be perfect — you need to be consistent. Show up 80% of the time, eat well 80% of the time, and the results will come. The other 20% is life, and that's okay. Trust the process, enjoy your food, and remember — every single session counts.
